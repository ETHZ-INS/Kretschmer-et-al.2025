# Functional glucocorticoid receptors in mature sperm contribute to the transmission of trauma effects across generations


Excessive stress influences disease risk across generations, yet the molecular pathways conveying paternal experiences remain unclear. Here, we identify the glucocorticoid receptor (GR), a major stress hormone receptor, as a previously unrecognized component of mature sperm. Using biochemical, chromatin-profiling and genetic labeling approaches, we show that GR occupies specific genomic loci in sperm and is transmitted to the embryo at fertilization. Protein-level manipulation demonstrates that GR contributes to early embryonic transcription. In a mouse model of acute traumatic stress, paternal stress alters glucocorticoid levels, sperm GR occupancy, embryonic and adult offspring gene expression, and offspring phenotypes. PROTAC-mediated depletion of sperm GR attenuates these effects. Finally, human sperm from men with high stress experiences exhibits alterations at identified stress-responsive GR-associated loci. Together, our findings implicate paternal GR signaling in the intergenerational transmission of stress effects and support conservation of stress-responsive GR biology in sperm from mice and humans.
